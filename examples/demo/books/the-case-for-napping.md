# The Case for Napping

## Why We Nap

### A Short History

Midday rest is older than the factory clock. Farming households across many regions paused through the hottest hours, and the practice only looked strange once work moved indoors and onto fixed shifts.

When I began writing about sleep, I assumed the afternoon slump was a sign of poor habits. I now think it is a feature of the human body that modern schedules ignore.

### The Afternoon Dip

Most adults feel a drop in alertness in the early afternoon, whether or not they have eaten lunch. In a laboratory study of 48 adults kept on a fixed schedule, reaction times slowed by about 15 percent between one and three in the afternoon, even when meals were skipped.

The dip is real, and fighting it with caffeine only postpones the cost.

## How to Nap Well

### Keep It Short

A nap of 10 to 20 minutes is the sweet spot. In a randomised trial of 120 office workers, a 20-minute nap improved afternoon memory scores by 12 percent compared with a rest period spent awake.

Naps longer than 30 minutes let you fall into deep sleep, and waking from deep sleep leaves you groggy for up to half an hour.

### Timing Matters

Nap before three in the afternoon. A late nap borrows from the night ahead.

In my own experience, a nap at two o'clock left me sharper in the evening than a second cup of coffee ever did.

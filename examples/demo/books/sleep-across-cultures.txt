Title: Sleep Across Cultures
Author: Ines Carvalho

Chapter 1: The Siesta

In the towns where I did my fieldwork, the afternoon rest was a social institution as much as a biological one. Shops closed, streets emptied, and families ate together before resting.

Older residents told me the siesta was what let them work late into the cool evening. Younger residents with office jobs had mostly given it up, and several said they missed it.

Chapter 2: What the Numbers Say

A survey of 9,000 adults in twelve countries found that regular nappers reported higher life satisfaction, but also slept about 40 minutes less at night. Whether naps cause shorter nights, or short nights cause naps, the survey cannot say.

My view is that the nap is neither good nor bad in itself. What matters is whether a culture's whole daily rhythm makes room for it.
